## Background

When you move to a new city, it forces a total filter of your physical anchors. Because I don't have many belongings with me right now, it made me reflect on the everyday items I do have. And this led me to a core question: when everything isfiltered, what is truly ‘essential’ to carry into anew life. 

And I realized the most precious things I possess are my sensory memories. So I chose some of my everyday items to represent human's 5 sensory.

## Select objects: the definition of "essential," and the sensory memories I want to keep.

To preserve my sensory memories, I didn't just want to curate a sterile list of artifacts. I chose four everyday objects that deeply anchor my personal life:

![Sight, hearing, smell, and taste: the four objects and why I kept them](/images/garden/time-capsule/objects-curated.png)

The sense of **touch**, however, was the hardest for me to package. Placing a textured object inside the box felt too literal. I gradually realized that touch shouldn't be an item stored *inside* the container—it should dictate how someone interacts with the container *itself*.

To make the curation of the five senses more deliberate and visible, I decided to add a shipping manifest label to the exterior. (I had originally planned to print a formal receipt, but all the printers were broken on a Sunday) I also added the strict instruction:*"Wear gloves when touching."* Alongside it, I attached a physical magnifier to invite people to lean in, physically interact,and look closer at the objects I was preserving.

## Container / Capsule

A cardboard shoebox. I have been packing my life in boxes like this. A delivery of 2026 to the future.

But I don't believe a plain shoebox would not survive 125 years, so I used expanding foam. In 2026 it is protective packing. In 2151 it looks mutated: thermal armor that swallowed the box.

## The future archaeologist
The future scenario and world-building inspiration came directly from my previous project, the 50 ways of seeing. For that assignment, I once tried to use [foil rubbings of the stones](https://stones-virid.vercel.app/ways/aluminum-foil-pressed), which I then brought into a game engine to use as a [digital terrain heightmap](https://stones-virid.vercel.app/ways/the-foil-heightmap). This sparked my future scenario: What if extreme climate collapse forced humanity to cover the entire surface of the Earth in a tensioned foil membrane to reflect lethal radiation?

- **Time:** 125 years ahead
- **Setting:** climate collapse. The Earth is a foil wasteland, from the foil rubbings in *50 Ways of Seeing*.
- **Discoverer:** not a person. An automated rover, scanning the surface.
